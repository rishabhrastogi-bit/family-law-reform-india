KNOW YOUR PATH

Before You Enter the System —
Understand How It Works.

Most people enter family court without knowing what’s coming. Here is a plain-language roadmap of the typical legal journey in a non-criminal marital dispute — from the first attempt at resolution to custody and divorce.

Two paths are shown: one for the husband’s side, one for the wife’s side. Both are legitimate. Both are here.

⚖️ Do You Need a Lawyer?

Option 1 — Hire an Advocate

The standard route. An advocate drafts your petition, appears on your behalf on most dates, advises strategy, and handles all court procedures.

Best when:
• Case is complex or contested
• Other side has a lawyer
• Criminal proceedings involved (498A, DV Act)
• You are unfamiliar with legal procedures
• You live far from the court

Option 2 — Party in Person (PiP)

You represent yourself directly in court — no advocate engaged. Legally called appearing as ‘Party in Person.’ Completely legal and recognised by all Indian courts.

Best when:
• Case is simple and uncontested
• Mutual consent divorce with full agreement
• You cannot afford an advocate
• You are confident handling paperwork and appearing yourself
• Other side is also unrepresented

Indian courts are legally obligated to assist Party in Person litigants. Judges and court staff must guide you on procedure if you ask. You have a right to file your own petition.

1
FIRST — TRY THIS

Pre-Litigation Mediation

Before filing anything in court

What it is

A structured, confidential conversation between both spouses facilitated by a trained mediator — before any case is filed in court. Completely voluntary. Nothing said here can be used against you in court.

Why to do it first

Saves years of litigation. Preserves co-parenting relationships. Cheaper. Faster. And courts often push you back here anyway.

Where to go

📍 Delhi — Samadhan Centre

Delhi HC Mediation & Conciliation Centre (Samadhan) — High Court of Delhi, Sher Shah Road.
Email: samadhan@dhc.nic.in  •  Fee: ₹21,000 (₹1,000 admin + ₹20,000 mediator honorarium)
Process: File application → pay fee → mediator appointed → other side notified → sessions begin within days.

🌎 Across India

Every High Court has a mediation centre. Key ones: Bangalore (Karnataka HC) • Bombay HC (Maharashtra) • Madras HC (Tamil Nadu) • Calcutta HC (West Bengal) • Allahabad HC (UP) • Supreme Court Mediation Centre (national).
Also: Lok Adalats and District Legal Services Authorities (DLSA) in every district offer free mediation.

The Mediation Act 2023 provides a national framework — making mediated settlements legally enforceable.

Typical timeline & outcomes ✅ Settlement → consent divorce or deed ❌ No settlement → proceed to Step 2 2–8 weeks typical
Mediation Act 2023 dhcmediation.nic.in

YES — and this is the easiest step to do yourself.

Samadhan Centre (Delhi HC): Visit in person: Delhi High Court Complex, Sher Shah Road, New Delhi 110003. Timing: 10am–1pm, Mon–Fri. Collect application form • Fill with both parties’ details and dispute description • Pay ₹21,000 Demand Draft in favour of ‘Registrar General, Delhi High Court’ • Submit and receive acknowledgment slip • Mediator appointed within days.

DLSA (free mediation): Visit your District Court complex, ask for the DLSA office, request ‘pre-litigation mediation’ form. No fee. Even simpler than Samadhan.

Documents to carry: Identity proof • Marriage certificate copy • Address proof • Brief written note of your dispute (1 page is enough)

You do NOT need a lawyer to initiate mediation.

2
IF SPOUSE LEFT

RCR — Restitution of Conjugal Rights

Section 9, Hindu Marriage Act 1955

What it is

A formal petition asking the court to direct your spouse to return to the matrimonial home. Not about forcing anyone — courts cannot physically compel cohabitation. The real purpose is legal documentation of the separation and creating a path to divorce if reconciliation fails.

Who can file

Either husband OR wife can file RCR. File in Family Court where the marriage took place, or where both spouses last resided together.

What happens

1. File petition in Family Court →  2. Court sends notice to spouse →  3. Mandatory mediation / counselling attempted →  4. If mediation fails, court hears evidence →  5. RCR decree granted if spouse left without valid reason →  6. Spouse has 1 year to comply →  7. Non-compliance for 1 year = automatic ground for divorce under Section 13(1A)(ii) HMA.

Strategic value

RCR is often filed as a legal record of desertion rather than a genuine expectation of return. The 1-year non-compliance clock starts from the decree date — giving a clear, documented path to divorce. The RCR decree can also serve as evidence of non-cooperation in subsequent GWA custody proceedings.

Timeline 1–3 years for decree +1 year non-compliance clock after decree
§9 HMA — RCR §13(1A) HMA — Divorce after RCR

YES — you can file an RCR petition yourself.

Draft the petition. Standard Family Court format: Title • Both parties’ details • Facts (married/separated/circumstances) • Prayer (direction to return to matrimonial home) • Verification affidavit. Templates at district court help desks, ecourts.gov.in, or DLSA.

File at Family Court counter. Print 3 copies on plain A4 • Sign every page • Write at top: ‘Petitioner appearing in Person’ (no vakalatnama needed) • Carry marriage certificate (orig + 2 copies), identity proof, address proof, court fee ₹98 in Delhi (stamps at counter). Clerk gives case number and first date.

First date: Tell the judge: ‘Your Honour, I am appearing as party in person.’

⚠️ If the other side hires an advocate, consider getting legal help at that stage.

2B
IF CHILD INVOLVED

Section 26 HMA — Interim Custody Orders

Hindu Marriage Act — file this within your HMA proceeding immediately

What it is

Section 26 allows the court to pass interim orders for custody, maintenance, and education of children while any HMA proceeding (RCR or divorce) is pending. Does not require a separate petition — filed as an application within your existing case.

What to ask for

• Defined visitation schedule (specific days, times, duration)
• Interim co-parenting access
• Right to attend school events and medical appointments
• Information rights — school records, health updates

File this on day one

Section 26 is your fastest route to court-ordered access while the main case is pending. File it simultaneously with your RCR or divorce petition — do not wait.

§26 HMA — Custody in Proceedings

YES — file as an application within your existing RCR/divorce case.

Format: ‘Application under Section 26 HMA for interim custody/visitation orders’
• Heading: case number of existing HMA case • To: Presiding Judge, Family Court [City] • Para 1: Brief facts • Para 2: Child details (name, age, address) • Para 3: What access/custody you seek • Para 4: Why in child’s best interest • Prayer • Verification + signature

File at same counter as your main case. Application fee: ₹25–50 process fee. No separate court fee stamp needed in most states.

3
CHILD ACCESS & CUSTODY

GWA — Guardians and Wards Act 1890

Sections 7, 25, and 12 — file all three together on the same day

What each section does

Section 7 — Appointment of Guardian

Petition to the court to declare you as the legal guardian of your child. This is the main / primary petition — everything else flows from this. Filed in District Court where the child ordinarily resides.

Section 25 — Custody of Minor

Allows a guardian to claim the return of a minor child who has been removed or kept away without permission. Filed together with Section 7 — strengthens the case for physical access and co-parenting.

File on Day One

Section 12 — Interim Application

Apply for urgent temporary orders while Sections 7 & 25 are pending (which can take years). This is how you get court-ordered visitation within weeks, not years. File simultaneously with Sections 7 and 25.

Why file all three together

Section 7 establishes your claim as guardian. Section 25 addresses the immediate physical custody issue. Section 12 gets you temporary access while the court processes the main case.

Filing all three on the same day signals seriousness to the court, addresses both the long-term and immediate access question simultaneously, and starts the Section 12 clock running immediately.

What to include in your Section 12 application

Your Section 12 application should specify:
• Exact visitation schedule requested (days, timings, pickup/drop location)
• Child’s current age and daily routine
• Your bond with the child (documented moments, photographs, school involvement)
• Any evidence of parental alienation — missed calls, blocked communication, child being coached against you
• Proposed neutral meeting point if handover is contentious

What courts typically grant at Section 12 stage

Under 5 years: Judicial tendency is for child to stay with mother during this age. Typical interim order: defined weekend visits (e.g. Saturday 11am to Sunday 6pm), alternating festivals, video call rights on weekdays. Can be expanded if parental alienation is evidenced.

5–12 years: Child’s routine and schooling considered. Overnight visits more readily granted. Courts may appoint a child counsellor to assess the child’s wellbeing.

12+ years: Child’s stated preference carries increasing weight. Courts are less likely to override an older child’s expressed wishes.

Your Section 12 application becomes stronger if you can show:

✓ You were the primary caregiver or equally involved before separation
✓ Child is being alienated from you (no contact being facilitated)
✓ You have a stable home environment
✓ Child has been denied access for a significant period already

Timeline Section 12 interim: 4–12 weeks Main petition (S.7 + S.25) final: 2–5 years
§7 GWA §25 GWA §12 GWA — Interim Vivek Singh v Romani Singh (2017) Calcutta HC Guidelines (2025) Lahari Sakhamuri (2019)

YES — GWA petitions can be filed as Party in Person. Three documents to file together:

1. Main Petition (S.7 + S.25): Child details, your relationship to child, current custody situation, why court should appoint you as guardian, child’s best interest arguments, relief sought.

2. Section 12 Application (urgent): Current denial of access, duration, specific interim relief (exact days/times), why urgent, prayer for interim order.

3. Affidavit: Verifying all facts. Get notarized (₹50–100 at notary near court).

File at: District Court (not Family Court) of district where child resides. Ask for ‘Guardian Court’ or ‘Civil Court filing counter.’ Court fee: ₹50 approx.

⚠️ At S.12 urgent hearing: ‘Your Honour, I am the petitioner appearing in person. I have filed an urgent application under Section 12 for interim visitation. The child has been denied access for [X months]. I request an urgent date.’

MAINTENANCE

Responding to Maintenance Claims

CrPC Section 125 / BNSS Section 144

What to expect

Wife may file for interim maintenance under Section 125 CrPC (now Section 144 BNSS) at any stage — even before or during divorce proceedings. Courts can award interim maintenance pending final hearing.

How courts calculate

• Husband’s income and earning capacity  • Wife’s income and earning capacity  • Child’s needs  • Standard of living during marriage  • Duration of marriage

What to document

• Your actual salary slips and IT returns (3 years)  • Wife’s income, assets, and earning capacity  • Your existing financial obligations  • Child’s actual expenses

Key precedents

Parvin Kumar Jain v. Anju Jain (2024) — Supreme Court’s 8-point framework for alimony calculation.

Manish Jain v. Akanksha Jain (2017) — self-sufficient spouse cannot claim maintenance.

§125 CrPC / §144 BNSS Parvin Kumar Jain (2024) Manish Jain (2017)

YES — you can respond to a maintenance claim yourself.

File a written statement at the same Magistrate Court that sent you the notice. Format: cause title matching the petition • para-by-para response • your accurate income disclosure • denial of exaggerated claims • verification + signature.

Key documents: Salary slips (last 3 months) • IT returns (last 2 years) • Bank statements • Proof of your expenses (EMIs, rent, medical) • If self-employed: business accounts or nil-income affidavit.

⚠️ Appear at interim maintenance hearings. Courts can pass interim orders without hearing you if you don’t appear. Underreporting income that the other side can prove will damage your credibility throughout the case.

⚖️ Do You Need a Lawyer?

Option 1 — Hire an Advocate

The standard route. An advocate drafts your petition, appears on your behalf on most dates, advises strategy, and handles all court procedures.

Best when:
• Case is complex or contested
• Other side has a lawyer
• Criminal proceedings involved (498A, DV Act)
• You are unfamiliar with legal procedures
• You live far from the court

Option 2 — Party in Person (PiP)

You represent yourself directly in court — no advocate engaged. Legally called appearing as ‘Party in Person.’ Completely legal and recognised by all Indian courts.

Best when:
• Case is simple and uncontested
• Mutual consent divorce with full agreement
• You cannot afford an advocate
• You are confident handling paperwork and appearing yourself
• Other side is also unrepresented

Indian courts are legally obligated to assist Party in Person litigants. Judges and court staff must guide you on procedure if you ask. You have a right to file your own petition.

CRIMINAL ROUTE

Section 498A IPC / Section 85 BNS

If there is cruelty or dowry harassment

What it covers

Section 498A IPC (now Section 85 BNS) covers cruelty by husband or his relatives — physical, mental, or related to dowry demands.

⚠ What to know before filing

498A is a non-bailable, cognizable offence. Filing it means immediate police action and potential arrest. Courts have repeatedly warned against filing if the situation does not genuinely warrant criminal action — false or exaggerated 498A cases are increasingly being treated as misconduct. File only when the situation is genuinely criminal.

Process

File FIR at the police station in the area where the incident occurred or where you currently reside.

§498A IPC / §85 BNS Arnesh Kumar v State of Bihar (2014)

498A FIR: FREE — file at police station, no court fee.

Free legal aid: DLSA provides free advocates — women qualify regardless of income.

Private advocate fees: ₹5,000 — ₹50,000+ for initial filing • ₹2,000 — ₹10,000 per hearing

Important: This is a criminal proceeding. Take legal advice before filing — you cannot easily withdraw a 498A case once filed.

Police investigation: Your presence is required for recording your statement.

Magistrate Court: You are not required at every hearing — your advocate can appear on your behalf for many dates.

Evidence / cross-examination stage: Your presence is required.

498A cases are heard in Magistrate Court — a criminal court. Dress conservatively, follow the same etiquette as family court.

498A / S.85 BNS is a CRIMINAL complaint — you file an FIR at a police station, not a petition in court.

How to file an FIR yourself: Go to the police station where the incident occurred or where you reside • Ask to file FIR under S.498A IPC / S.85 BNS • Give written complaint or dictate to officer • Ensure FIR is registered and you receive a copy (your legal right) • If police refuse: complain to Superintendent of Police or approach Magistrate under S.156(3) CrPC / S.175(3) BNSS.

⚠️ Critical warning: 498A is non-compoundable — you cannot easily withdraw it once filed. Think carefully before filing. Take legal advice first if possible.

DLSA free legal aid: Women qualify regardless of income. Apply at your district legal services authority for a free advocate throughout the criminal proceeding.

What Happens to the Child During All of This?

Regardless of which path either parent takes, the child’s welfare is the court’s primary focus. No parent should be erased from a child’s life without proven cause.

If you are the father

  • File Section 26 immediately within your HMA proceeding
  • File Sections 7, 25, and 12 GWA together on the same day in District Court — do not file them separately or at different times
  • Section 12 is your fastest path to court-ordered access
  • Document every missed call, blocked message, and refused visit
  • Never speak negatively about the mother in front of the child

If you are the mother

  • Comply with any court-ordered visitation — violations are increasingly treated seriously
  • Understand that parental alienation is now recognised as harm to the child
  • Courts are moving toward shared parenting — cooperation is in your child’s best interest
